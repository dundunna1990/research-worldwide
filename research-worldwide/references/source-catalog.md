**Updated:** 20 October 2026. **Authority:** Baseline Researcher_Skill_No_Key_Sources_2026-10-18.md: 142 PASSED, 0 FAILED, 0 NOT RESOLVED; 142 additional 2026-10-20 source verifications. This file is a concise source-routing guide, not SKILL.md or a test report.

**WORLDWIDE MULTILINGUAL RULE:** For a general subject question, search every approved source reasonably relevant to the subject across countries and languages. Skip only clearly unrelated sources. Never default to Canada, the United States or English. Translate concepts into local languages and original scripts **before** searching; read important findings in the original language, translate them afterward, and **answer in English with original-source citations**. Expand internationally when evidence is missing, one-sided or conflicting. Route by subject and material coverage, not website hosting country.

**How to use each row:** The final column gives the tested public route and its limit. “Search” means the source accepted a topic query or public browse route; “known-item retrieval” means the skill must first obtain an identifier or link elsewhere; “item reading” means the listed record/document was opened but sitewide search was not proved. Never turn a tested record lookup into an invented keyword-search claim. A source listing, abstract, linked PDF, PDF retrieval and document actually read are different evidence levels. Do not bypass bot blocks, sign-in or paywalls. Deduplicate the same underlying study across indexes and respect publication rights.

**Routing rule:** Section headings are navigation aids, not hard boundaries. For broad research, also inspect general discovery, regional/multilingual collections, repositories and finding aids where they could contain relevant work. A tested example shows that a route works; it does **not** restrict a repository to that example's topic, country or language.

**Discussion caution:** All community rows are labeled **DISCUSSION / EXPERIENCE / LEAD — VERIFY IMPORTANT FACTS**. They are useful for firsthand reports, practical terms, public opinion, disagreement, new leads or original document links, **not automatic factual proof**. Watch for anonymous posts, moderation, bots, deletions and misleading votes; verify material claims with original research, records, laws, standards or reliable datasets. Stack Exchange subjects and languages are separate routing entry points on one network, not independent corroborations.
